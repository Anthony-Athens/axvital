## Calculation policy version 1

`lib/experiments/observational-results.ts` centralizes eligibility, summaries, interpretation and overlap. A pair needs a finite valid recorded outcome of the study's immutable metric, its supported scale/version, current aligned Sprint 3 evidence, supported status/measurement and compatible units. Zero is a valid outcome. Not-observed, missing, invalid, unknown, not-applicable, needs-review, incomplete, errored and non-current evidence have explicit exclusions. Unknown is never included in either binary group. Reliable Present may be usable with incomplete overall logging; Absent requires the adapter's completeness. Diet exceptions retain their own category, and unresolved diets remain data-quality categories.

Binary comparisons use Present versus confirmed Absent. Diet comparisons use Adherent versus Nonadherent, with Planned exception summarized separately. Numeric outcomes show mean, median, units and recorded-value distributions; differences are first minus second means. Ordinal outcomes prioritize median and distribution with user anchors; differences are median rating differences, without assuming equal clinical meaning between steps. Yes/no outcomes show Yes numerator/denominator and proportion; differences are percentage points. All observations are visible at any sample size. A directional sentence requires **at least five usable observations in both regular comparison groups**. Insufficient-data messages name both counts; constant factors explicitly report lack of variation. Outcome direction preferences are described without asserting benefit or harm.

Continuous factors use exact canonical aligned measurements (verified weight already normalized to kg by Sprint 3). Scatter plots and calendar trends have exact table alternatives. No interpolation, carry-forward, high/low cutoffs or smoothing enters comparisons. Optional Spearman rank correlation requires **at least ten usable pairs and variation in both variables**; average ranks handle ties, then Pearson correlation of those ranks gives rho. Constants and insufficient pairs suppress rho with a reason. Yes/no outcomes instead show factor distributions by Yes/No outcome. Correlation is descriptive, without p-values or significance/confidence badges.

Alcohol and supplement presence analyses are separate from optional known-quantity analyses. Known totals use only Sprint 3 amounts and supported units. Unknown totals remain excluded, including confirmed absence without a quantified total; no zero dose is invented. Different mass/count/volume units are separate segments. Supplement results describe recorded use, never effectiveness or recommended treatment.

## Segmentation, overlap and time

Comparisons conservatively segment by canonical factor identity, offset, immutable factor configuration ID, applicable diet rule-version ID, status/amount measurement, units and calculation version. Even unchanged factors are not pooled across a configuration change. Each study has one immutable outcome definition; incompatible metric IDs/unsupported versions are excluded rather than pooled. Existing scales cannot be edited; a new scale requires another canonical metric. Cross-segment counts are not presented as one pooled relationship.

Binary overlap uses outcome dates with a valid outcome and current known evidence for both aligned factors. It reports both present, first only, second only, neither, and excluded counts, separately for configurations and alignments. **Fewer than five discordant dates**, or **at least 90% both-present among dates with either present**, triggers the limited independent variation message. These are display rules, not statistical identifiability estimates. No-known-pair segments supply counts without a reassuring interpretation.

If comparison groups occupy disjoint calendar intervals, a deterministic message names their dates and explains that time cannot be separated from the comparison. Continuous-factor and outcome trends make other timing changes inspectable. No automated claim that weight/treatment/time is controlled is made. Directional wording/correlation is withheld for a segment containing retrieval errors; valid observed summaries and exclusion counts remain inspectable. No LLM, trigger ranking, disease-specific logic, multivariable adjustment or causal interpretation is used.

## Reads, authorization and integrity

`readObservationalResults` in `lib/experiments/observational-results-service.ts` reads owner-filtered outcome records and calls `readLinkedEvidence` in sequential windows of at most 31 dates. Source alignment, exposures, supplement dose normalization, composites and Diets assessments are never reconstructed. At most twelve chunks cover a study; source promises are reused within each chunk, with at most a one-day preceding-source boundary reread. Within each chunk, at most four dates run together while sharing source promises and retaining date order. The client sends one bounded results request, not a per-date/factor request. Default windows keep review inexpensive; long-range latency should be measured in staging before release.

The new GET `/api/experiments/observational-results?id=…&start=…&end=…` authorizes study ownership, uses the existing database API budget, enforces `full_experiments` entitlement, and returns no-store results. RLS remains authoritative. Existing observation/history reads, intervention results, billing, logging, export and deletion implementations remain intact. Results are ephemeral: no new table, migration, export record, analytics event or diagnostic health log. Nothing was applied to production or deployed, and no personal data/experiments/enrollments/supplement choices were created.

Each source snapshot is coherent, but the outcome and domain/window reads are separate transactions. This is a fresh descriptive read, not an immutable cross-domain capture. Source metadata caps fail closed. Whole-request failure clears prior output; focus/manual refresh clears stale results while fetching. This feature introduces no realtime subscription or cached result store.
